### **Summary (English)**  
This study identifies a **55-Mb double-inversion supergene on chromosome Omy05** in **rainbow trout (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*)**, which regulates **sex-specific migratory behavior** through **sex-dependent dominance reversal**. Unlike sex chromosomes, which resolve sexual conflicts but accumulate deleterious mutations, this supergene maintains **alternative migratory tactics (resident vs. anadromous)** while avoiding mutation load. The **supergene contains genes related to circadian rhythm, adiposity, and reproduction**, and its frequency varies with **latitude and environmental selection**. These findings reveal a novel autosomal mechanism for resolving **sexual conflict over migration** while maintaining **genetic diversity and fitness variation** in salmonid populations.

### **Summary of "Detection of Growth-Related QTL in Turbot (Scophthalmus maximus)" (Approx. 150 Words)**  

Turbot (**Scophthalmus maximus**) is a commercially valuable flatfish in European aquaculture, with growth traits being a primary focus of breeding programs. This study aimed to detect **quantitative trait loci (QTL)** influencing **body weight, length, and Fulton’s condition factor** using a panel of **100 microsatellite markers** covering 26 linkage groups (LGs).  

Researchers analyzed **814 individuals from eight families** using **maximum likelihood and regression interval mapping**. They identified **11 significant QTLs**, including:  
- **Four for body weight** (LGs 5, 14, 15, 16),  
- **Five for length** (LGs 5, 6, 12, 14, 15),  
- **Two for Fulton’s condition factor** (LGs 3, 16).  

These QTLs provide potential targets for **marker-assisted selection (MAS)**. However, **high variability** among families suggests the need for **fine mapping and functional validation** to enhance breeding efficiency in turbot aquaculture.

### **Summary of "Uncovering QTL for Resistance and Survival Time to *Philasterides dicentrarchi* in Turbot (*Scophthalmus maximus*)" (Approx. 150 Words)**  

Disease resistance is a key trait in aquaculture breeding, particularly for turbot (*Scophthalmus maximus*), which is highly susceptible to scuticociliatosis caused by *Philasterides dicentrarchi*. This study aimed to identify **quantitative trait loci (QTL)** associated with **disease resistance and survival time** using genome-wide scans in four turbot families.  

Two statistical approaches—**linear regression (LR) and maximum likelihood (ML)**—were used to map QTL across **26 linkage groups (LGs)**. Key findings include:  
- **Significant QTL for resistance** on **LG1, LG9, and LG23**.  
- **Significant QTL for survival time** on **LG6, LG9, and LG23**.  
- Some markers explained up to **22% of phenotypic variance**.  

These QTLs provide **potential targets for marker-assisted selection (MAS)**, aiding genetic improvement for disease resistance in turbot aquaculture. Further research is needed to refine candidate genes and improve breeding programs.

### **Summary of "Disentangling Genetic Variation for Resistance and Endurance to Scuticociliatosis in Turbot Using Pedigree and Genomic Information" (Approx. 150 Words)**  

Selective breeding for disease resilience in turbot (*Scophthalmus maximus*) is crucial for aquaculture, particularly against *Philasterides dicentrarchi*, which causes scuticociliatosis. This study aimed to distinguish **resistance** (avoiding infection) and **endurance** (surviving despite infection) using genomic and pedigree data from 1,800 fish across 44 families.  

Key findings include:  
- **Heritability estimates**: 0.26 for resistance, 0.12 for endurance, and 0.15 for resilience.  
- A **high genetic correlation (0.90) between resistance and resilience**, indicating shared genetic mechanisms.  
- **A significant QTL on linkage group 19** explaining 33% of genetic variance in resilience, linked to immune-related genes.  
- **Genomic selection improved accuracy by 12%** over pedigree-based methods.  

This study provides **valuable genetic insights** for breeding disease-resistant turbot, with potential applications in **marker-assisted selection (MAS) and genomic selection (GS)**.

### **Summary of "Genomic Signatures After Five Generations of Intensive Selective Breeding: Runs of Homozygosity and Genetic Diversity in Representative Domestic and Wild Populations of Turbot (*Scophthalmus maximus*)" (Approx. 150 Words)**  

Selective breeding in aquaculture influences genomic diversity and inbreeding. This study analyzed **genomic signatures of selection in turbot (*Scophthalmus maximus*)** by examining **runs of homozygosity (ROH) and genetic diversity** in **domestic and wild populations**. Using **SNP genotyping data**, researchers identified **49 ROH islands in the domestic population but none in wild populations**, indicating increased homozygosity due to selective breeding.  

Key findings include:  
- **Genomic inbreeding (FROH) was higher in domestic turbot**, reflecting selective pressure on growth and disease resistance traits.  
- **Regions with lower genetic diversity were enriched for QTLs associated with growth, disease resistance, and metabolism**, highlighting selective sweep events.  
- **Wild turbot retained higher genetic variability**, while **domestication led to a loss of rare alleles**.  

This study provides valuable insights for **breeding program optimization**, suggesting **marker-assisted selection (MAS) and genomic selection (GS)** can help balance genetic diversity and inbreeding in farmed turbot.

### **Summary of "Highly Dense Linkage Maps from 31 Full-Sibling Families of Turbot (*Scophthalmus maximus*) Provide Insights into Recombination Patterns and Chromosome Rearrangements Throughout a Newly Refined Genome Assembly" (Approx. 150 Words)**  

High-density genetic maps are essential for understanding genome organization and improving selective breeding in aquaculture species. This study constructed a **high-resolution linkage map for turbot (*Scophthalmus maximus*)** using **2b-RAD sequencing** on **1,268 individuals from 31 full-sibling families**, identifying **18,214 SNP markers**.  

Key findings include:  
- A **consensus linkage map** with **11,845 markers across 22 linkage groups (LGs)**, covering **3,753.9 cM**.  
- **Male recombination rates were lower than female rates**, particularly around centromeres.  
- The **turbot genome assembly was refined**, with 96% of the genome ordered into 22 chromosomes.  
- **Chromosomal rearrangements and polymorphisms** were identified in specific regions.  
- **Comparative genomics** with other flatfish species provided insights into **chromosome evolution and rearrangements**.  

These results enhance **genome-assisted breeding strategies** for turbot, improving selection efficiency for key traits such as **growth and disease resistance**.

### **Summary of "Integration of Host-Pathogen Functional Genomics Data into the Chromosome-Level Genome Assembly of Turbot (*Scophthalmus maximus*)" (Approx. 150 Words)**

This study integrates **host-pathogen genomic data** into the latest **chromosome-level genome assembly** to improve selective breeding for disease resistance. Researchers reannotated **differentially expressed genes (DEGs)** from various pathogen infections (**Aeromonas salmonicida, Philasterides dicentrarchi, Enteromyxum scophthalmi, and Viral Hemorrhagic Septicemia Virus**), identified **quantitative trait loci (QTLs)**, and mapped **runs of homozygosity (ROH)**.  

Key findings include:  
- **81.1% of DEGs were successfully mapped** to the genome, revealing immune-related pathways (chemotaxis, apoptosis, leukocyte differentiation).  
- **Shared DEGs (hamp, plg, fga-like) were linked to multiple pathogens and QTLs**, highlighting common resistance mechanisms.  
- **QTLs associated with disease resistance and growth** were integrated into the genome for marker-assisted selection.  

This comprehensive genomic atlas **enhances breeding strategies** to improve turbot disease resilience and provides a valuable resource for genomic selection in aquaculture.

### **Summary of "A genome-wide association study, supported by a new chromosome-level genome assembly, suggests sox2 as a main driver of the undifferentiated ZZ/ZW sex determination of turbot (*Scophthalmus maximus*)" (Approx. 150 Words)**  

Sex determination (SD) in fish is highly diverse, often involving undifferentiated sex chromosomes. This study presents a **new chromosome-level genome assembly** for turbot (*Scophthalmus maximus*), enabling the identification of key genetic components involved in its **ZZ/ZW sex determination system**. Using a **genome-wide association study (GWAS) across 36 full-sibling families**, researchers identified a **6.8 Mb SD region on chromosome 12**, where **sox2** emerged as the strongest candidate gene.  

Key findings include:  
- **sox2 was differentially expressed between sexes before gonad differentiation**, suggesting a role in ovarian development.  
- **A single SNP** showed consistent differences between **Z and W chromosomes**, supporting genetic differentiation.  
- **Additional genetic and environmental factors** influence SD, leading to interfamily variation.  

This study provides new insights into fish SD evolution, highlighting **sox2 as a key regulator** in turbot, with potential applications for **all-female population production in aquaculture**.

### **Summary of "Identification of Stress-Related Genes by Co-Expression Network Analysis Based on the Improved Turbot Genome" (Approx. 150 Words)**  

Turbot (*Scophthalmus maximus*) is an economically significant aquaculture species facing various environmental and disease-related stresses. This study presents a **high-quality, chromosome-scale genome assembly** using **PacBio long-read and Illumina short-read sequencing**, improving genome completeness and annotation.  

Key findings:  
- The genome assembly spans **538.22 Mb with 22,442 protein-coding genes and 3,345 ncRNAs**.  
- **345 stress-responsive genes** were identified through **co-expression network analysis** based on **14 stress-related RNA-seq datasets (165 samples)**.  
- Stress factors analyzed include **pathogens (Enteromyxum scophthalmi, Vibrio anguillarum), heat, oxygen, crowding, salinity, and feeding stress**.  
- **Key hub genes associated with stress responses** were discovered, providing insights into stress resistance mechanisms.  

This study enhances understanding of **turbot’s genetic response to environmental stressors**, offering a valuable resource for **genomic research and selective breeding programs** to improve disease resistance and aquaculture sustainability.

### **Summary of "Integrative Transcriptome, Genome and Quantitative Trait Loci Resources Identify Single Nucleotide Polymorphisms in Candidate Genes for Growth Traits in Turbot" (Approx. 150 Words)**

Key findings:  
- **Muscle and liver RNA-seq** of 18 individuals identified **20,447 genes and 85,344 SNPs**.  
- **Co-localization analysis** placed **45 growth-related SNPs** near known **QTL markers**.  
- **43 SNPs were validated in a wild Atlantic population**, where **91% were polymorphic**.  
- Growth-related genes included **insulin-like growth factors (IGF1, IGF2), leptin receptor (LEPR), and myogenin (MYOG)**.  
- Findings support **marker-assisted selection (MAS)** and **population genetics studies** in turbot.  

This study provides **valuable genomic resources for selective breeding**, improving **growth traits and aquaculture sustainability**.

### **Summary of "Multi-Tissue scRNA-seq Reveals Immune Cell Landscape of Turbot (*Scophthalmus maximus*)" (Approx. 100 Words)**  

This study investigates the **immune cell landscape of turbot** (*Scophthalmus maximus*) using **single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq)** across multiple immune tissues (**head kidney, spleen, gills, and posterior intestine**). Researchers identified **16 distinct immune cell types**, including **T and B cell subsets, dendritic cells (DCs), macrophages (MΦs), and neutrophils**. **CD8+ cytotoxic T cells, Th17 cells, and innate lymphoid cells (ILCs)** displayed **dynamic immune responses** during bacterial infection. The study provides a **comprehensive fish immune atlas**, offering insights into **adaptive immunity evolution** and aiding in **vaccine development for aquaculture**.  

### **《多组织单细胞RNA测序揭示大菱鲆免疫细胞图谱》摘要（约100字）**  

本研究利用 **单细胞RNA测序（scRNA-seq）** 解析 **大菱鲆（*Scophthalmus maximus*）的免疫细胞图谱**，覆盖 **头肾、脾脏、鳃和后肠** 等免疫组织。研究共鉴定 **16 种免疫细胞类型**，包括 **T/B 细胞亚群、树突状细胞（DCs）、巨噬细胞（MΦs）和中性粒细胞**。**CD8+ 细胞毒性T细胞、Th17细胞和固有淋巴细胞（ILCs）** 在**细菌感染期间表现出动态免疫应答**。本研究构建了 **全面的鱼类免疫细胞图谱**，有助于 **揭示适应性免疫进化**，并为 **水产疫苗开发** 提供重要数据。
