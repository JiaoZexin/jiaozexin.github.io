### **Summary of the Publication (150 Words)**

This study presents proteomic data from European seabass (**Dicentrarchus labrax**) juveniles fed methionine-enriched diets for 18 and 85 days. Using label-free shotgun proteomics, liver protein expression was analyzed to understand the metabolic and physiological impacts of varying methionine levels (0.77%, 1%, 1.36%, and 1.66% Met w/w). Liver samples were processed via LC-MS/MS, and differential abundant proteins (DAP) were identified through statistical analyses, including One-Way and Two-Way ANOVA. The findings highlight significant changes in protein expression associated with methionine intake, revealing metabolic pathways involved in nutrient utilization, immune response, and cellular regulation. The study provides a valuable dataset for refining methionine dietary requirements in aquaculture, improving fish health and growth performance. The raw proteomic data are publicly accessible for further research on metabolic adaptation in teleost fish.

### **Summary (150 words)**  
This study investigates the effects of methionine-enriched diets on the liver proteome and growth performance of European seabass (*Dicentrarchus labrax*) using label-free shotgun proteomics. Methionine (Met) is essential for fish metabolism, growth, and immune function, especially in plant-based diets. A dose-dependent response to dietary Met was observed, with optimal growth at ≥0.9% Met (w/w). Proteomics data suggest an ideal nutritional status at 1.36% Met, while immune system benefits extend up to 1.66% Met. Key metabolic and immune pathways, including mTOR, Hedgehog, and T-cell receptor signaling, were activated. Fish at lower Met levels exhibited increased proteins involved in homeostasis, while higher Met levels promoted protein translation and immune enhancement. The study highlights the potential of proteomics to refine dietary Met requirements in aquaculture, demonstrating that methionine plays a critical role in both growth and immunity without triggering undesirable inflammatory responses.

### **Summary of "Transcriptomic Analysis of Liver Tissue of Black Sea Bass (*Centropristis striata*) Exposed to High Nitrogen Environment" (Approx. 150 Words)**  

The black sea bass (*Centropristis striata*) is a valuable species for aquaculture, but **elevated nitrate levels in culture environments can cause metabolic and tissue damage**. This study used **RNA-seq analysis** to investigate the molecular response of black sea bass liver under nitrate stress.  

### **Key Findings:**  
- **8,920 genes showed differential expression**, with **2,949 upregulated and 5,971 downregulated** under nitrate stress.  
- Enrichment analysis revealed pathways related to **immune response, inflammation, and coagulation cascades**.  
- Genes associated with **lipid metabolism, blood microparticles, and enzyme activity regulation** were significantly affected.  
- The **complement and coagulation pathways** were activated, suggesting an immune response to environmental stress.  

These results highlight the **adverse effects of nitrate stress on liver function** and provide insights into the **molecular mechanisms of stress resistance** in black sea bass, aiding future aquaculture management and breeding strategies.

### **Summary of "Dietary Aquaculture By-Product Hydrolysates: Impact on the Transcriptomic Response of the Intestinal Mucosa of European Seabass (*Dicentrarchus labrax*) Fed Low Fish Meal Diets" (Approx. 150 Words)**  

With the projected increase in aquaculture production, sustainable alternatives to fish meal (FM) in aquafeeds are essential. This study evaluates the effects of **tilapia, shrimp, and mixed hydrolysates** on the **growth, intestinal morphology, and transcriptomic response** of European seabass (*Dicentrarchus labrax*) fed a **low FM diet (5%)** over 65 days. Growth performance, intestinal histology, and RNA-seq analysis were conducted.  

### **Key Findings:**  
- **Growth performance restoration:** Hydrolysate supplementation restored growth levels to match high FM diets (20%).  
- **Intestinal morphology improvement:** Shrimp hydrolysate increased **villi height** and **goblet cell density**, surpassing the control.  
- **Transcriptomic regulation:**  
  - **Shrimp hydrolysate enhanced immune responses**.  
  - **Tilapia hydrolysate influenced nutrient metabolism**.  
- **Synergistic effect of hydrolysate mixture:** Mixed hydrolysates regulated more genes and metabolic pathways than single hydrolysates.  

These findings suggest that **aquaculture by-product hydrolysates are a promising fish meal alternative**, supporting **fish health and sustainable feed development**.

### **Summary of "RNA-Seq analysis of European sea bass (*Dicentrarchus labrax*) infected with nodavirus reveals powerful modulation of the stress response" (Approx. 150 Words)**  

Nodavirus, or **nervous necrosis virus (NNV)**, is a major pathogen in **European sea bass (*Dicentrarchus labrax*)**, causing **viral encephalopathy and retinopathy (VER)** with **high mortality rates**. This study used **RNA-Seq** to analyze transcriptomic responses in the **brain and head kidney** of infected juveniles at **24 and 72 hours post-infection (hpi)**. Contrary to expectations, **immune gene activation was modest**, and some were even **downregulated** in the brain. However, **stress response genes** were strongly modulated, particularly those involved in **cortisol synthesis** in the head kidney at **24 hpi**. This stress response diminished at **72 hpi**, coinciding with a progressive **immune response**. Further analysis revealed that **stress activation influenced NNV replication**, demonstrating a complex interplay between **stress, viral infection, and immunity**. These findings suggest that **stress modulation** could be a key factor in disease susceptibility and progression in sea bass aquaculture.

### **Summary of "Assessing the Economic Impact of Diseases in Mediterranean Grow-Out Farms Culturing European Sea Bass" (Approx. 100 Words)**  

This study evaluates the **economic impact of diseases** in **Mediterranean European sea bass (*Dicentrarchus labrax*) farms** using a **deterministic static model**. The analysis considers **different farm sizes and production strategies** to estimate **direct costs of disease outbreaks** caused by pathogens such as **nodavirus (VER) and vibriosis**. Results indicate that **small farms and farms producing smaller fish suffer greater economic losses** due to reduced profit margins. Disease outbreaks **increase mortality, reduce growth rates, and worsen feed conversion ratios**, emphasizing the need for **preventive measures like vaccination and biosecurity** to sustain farm profitability.  

### **《地中海欧洲海鲈养殖场疾病的经济影响评估》摘要（约100字）**  

本研究采用 **确定性静态模型** 评估 **地中海欧洲海鲈（*Dicentrarchus labrax*）养殖场疾病的经济影响**，分析 **不同养殖规模和生产策略** 下，由 **结节病毒（VER）和弧菌病** 引起的 **疾病爆发的直接成本**。研究发现，**小型养殖场和生产较小规格鱼的养殖场** 经济损失更严重，因其利润空间较低。**疾病爆发导致死亡率上升、生长速率下降、饲料转化率恶化**，强调 **疫苗接种和生物安全等预防措施** 对维持养殖场盈利能力的必要性。
